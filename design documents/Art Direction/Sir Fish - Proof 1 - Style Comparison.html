What this proof does and doesn't settle

It settles the tonal question

Whether panel edges, wells and the world/frame boundary survive a dim screen is purely a question of luminance relationships, and those are identical here and in the engine. Your eye adapts within seconds, so judging three dark palettes sequentially — loading three builds one after another — is unreliable. Seeing them simultaneously is the only accurate comparison, and no set of Godot builds can do that on one phone.

It doesn't settle the render question

There is no 3D here: no cel shading, fog, glow, or ink outlines. Storybook in particular is under-represented, because most of what makes it Storybook lives in the render pass (§4 of its spec), not the palette. Treat B's swatch as its colour argument only.

Motion, animation and how the meshes actually read are out of scope entirely.

Type and palette are separated on purpose

The Type & borders switch applies to all three proofs at once. That is deliberate: if you compared Lantern-with-Phase-0 against current-as-shipped, you'd credit the palette for what the type ramp did. Flip it and watch how much of the improvement arrives before any colour changes.

Recommended next step

Use this to eliminate, not to choose. If a style fails here it will fail in the engine; if it survives, it earns a real build. Then spike the survivor's palette only — tuning.gd §6.1 is one file — and push that to the style-test itch project rather than implementing either spec in full.
