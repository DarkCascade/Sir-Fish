Lantern in four hues

The Lantern structure with its console hue rotated. Every other value — the gold rim, the unlit night world, the violet modal, all text — is byte-identical across the four, so the console hue is the only variable.

All four pass the readability gate identically (chrome edges 2.03–2.09:1, world 2.07:1, text 3.72–3.77:1; the spread is 8-bit rounding). Hue was never the load-bearing variable — luminance was. So this is not a legibility choice, it is a semantic one: what each hue collides with.

Why the world doesn't change

A full art direction would tint the lit near-field toward the lamp — a purple lantern throws purple light onto the moss in front of it. That is deliberately not done here, because it would confound the thing being judged. What you're seeing is the lamp's own colour against a fixed night.

Whichever hue wins, budget a pass on C_GROUND and C_BRUSH to carry a hint of it.

The gold rim is fixed on purpose

§6.1's rule — gold is the UI — is the one relationship every variant keeps. Holding C_PANEL_BORDER constant at #BFA864 also isolates the variable: if the rim moved with the hue, you'd be judging two changes at once.

Ember is the one variant where this needs re-testing — a warm rim on a warm panel loses its edge.
